- **01/01/2026:** Allan Victor Robinson, 67-year-old man with hypertension and osteoarthritis, presented to A&E after a minor fall with acute confusion. He had no reported loss of consciousness, headache, vomiting, visual symptoms, chest pain, palpitations, or dizziness. Initial GCS was 12/15 with disorientation to time and place; BP was 170/95 and pulse 88 bpm. He had no known drug allergies and was not taking regular medications. Urgent blood tests and CT head were arranged to investigate intracranial injury; the documented ED diagnosis was **subdural hygroma**. Blood samples included FNC, electrolytes, and coagulation studies. A repeat CT head was planned for 48 hours later, with close neurological monitoring and MDT review if confusion persisted. Paracetamol 1 g four times daily was planned for headache.
- **01/01/2026:** Physiotherapy identified mild confusion and slight gait instability without significant falls risk; daily balance and mobility therapy was planned. Occupational therapy found mild impairment in fine motor coordination affecting buttons and utensils, with therapy planned for self-care and home adaptations. Family was updated regarding the admission and subdural haematoma/hygroma.
- **02/01/2026:** During physiotherapy, the patient completed an assisted corridor walk using a frame. Stability had slightly improved, although mild gait instability and confusion persisted. He remained cooperative and physiologically stable.
- **03/01/2026:** Balance and coordination improved with minimal assistance. GCS was 14/15, with equal reactive pupils. Stair-climbing exercises were planned.
- **04/01/2026:** He climbed and descended five steps using a handrail with minimal supervision and verbal cues. Mobility and balance continued to improve, with mild residual confusion. Social work referral, ongoing physiotherapy and occupational therapy, and discharge assessment were planned.
- **05/01/2026:** Atorvastatin 40 mg once daily was started as a 7-day course for cardiovascular risk. Final documented physiotherapy assessment showed resolution of confusion, GCS 15/15, independent walking for 15 metres, steady gait, and good balance and coordination. He was cleared for independent mobility, with home exercises recommended and no further physiotherapy required at that stage. Social work discussed discharge support and recommended community support services.
- **06/01/2026:** Education was provided regarding management of dizziness/confusion and falls prevention at home, with post-discharge safety advice.
- **07/01/2026:** The patient was clinically stable with no residual confusion or neurological deficits; GCS remained 15/15. He was independently mobile, including walking 15 metres, climbing five steps with a handrail, and navigating obstacles. He was deemed fit for discharge with no further physiotherapy needs, and a neurology clinic follow-up had been arranged. Discharge was planned for the following day.
